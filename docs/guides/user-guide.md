# CRMS user guide

For police officers using the Crime Records Management System. **This is a draft.** Workflows, roles and wording must be confirmed with operational stakeholders before training.

## The rules that always apply

- Use your own account. Never share it, and never leave a session open.
- Access only what you need for your duties. **Everything you view, search, change, print or export is recorded against your name.**
- A record is not a finding. Being linked to a case does not make someone guilty. Closing a case does not mean anyone was convicted or cleared.
- Do not put personal details in reasons and notes unless they are needed: these are copied into audit records.

## Signing in

You will receive an invitation link. Choose a password and link an authenticator app. Keep your recovery codes somewhere safe.

You are signed out after a period of inactivity, and again after a maximum session length. You are also signed out if your role or station changes.

If you lose your phone, ask your Security Administrator to reset your authenticator.

## What you can see

This depends on your role and where you are posted. Some examples:

- **Records Officer:** cases at your station.
- **Investigating Officer:** only the cases assigned to you.
- **Station Commander:** cases at your station, including restricted ones.
- **District Supervisor:** cases in your district.

If a page says "not found", the record either does not exist or is not available to you. The system does not say which.

## People

**Registering someone:** People → Register person. Collect only what is needed. An identity number starts as _unverified_, because having it on file proves nothing.

**Reading a record:** identity numbers, contact details and addresses are masked. If your role allows it, choose **Reveal**, state your purpose and re-enter your password. Each reveal is recorded. Close the window as soon as you have finished.

**Correcting a record:** choose **Correct details** and give a reason. The earlier value is kept.

## Cases

**Opening a case:** Cases → Open case. The case opens at your station and gets its reference automatically.

**Linking people:** on the case, choose **Link person**, find them, and choose their role in _this_ case. The same person can have different roles in different cases.

**Recording progress:** add notes and change the status from the case page. A reason is required, and everything stays in the history.

**Status is not an outcome.** "Closed administratively" is a police workflow state only.

**Archived records** are read-only. **Restricted records** are hidden from most of the station.

## Search

Search → choose case reference, identity number or name. Identity and name searches ask for your purpose.

- A **name match is only a possible match**. Do not treat it as the same person without checking.
- Results only include records you can see. "No results" does not mean a record does not exist.

## Asking for access to another case

Search → **Request access to a case**. Give the reference and explain why you need it. An authorised officer decides. If approved, your access is time-limited, recorded and reviewed. This is for exceptions, and must not become routine.

## Documents

On a case, choose **Upload** (PDF, JPEG, PNG or plain text). A file can be opened only after its malware scan has finished. These are case attachments, **not** an evidence chain-of-custody system.

## Court records

If your role allows it, add the proceeding and record the outcome from an authorised source such as a certified court record. **An outcome has no effect until a different officer verifies it.** Only a verified outcome creates a conviction record, and later outcomes such as a successful appeal are added rather than overwriting the earlier ones.

## Exports and printing

Exports, when enabled, contain case lists with no personal data. You must state a purpose, large exports need approval, and each file can be downloaded once. Printed copies are marked with your name and recorded. Keep them secure, and do not store them on shared computers.

## If something looks wrong

If you see a sign-in you do not recognise, change your password and report it. Report any suspected misuse of the system at once.
